Decoherence theory already explains why system properties become
definite. This paper asks what that explanation is worth to someone who
can tune the strength of an entangling gate. The Relational Coherence
Framework (\RCF) restates four standard identities of pointer-basis
coherence theory in terms of a normalized Hilbert--Schmidt coherence
quantifier fixed by einselection, and treats the coherence lost across
a circuit layer as a cost. None of this changes the predictions of
quantum mechanics. A relational reading of quantum mechanics serves as
the conceptual setting; I do not claim it is singled out among the
alternatives.

For tunable Heisenberg-exchange gates under Markovian dephasing
proportional to gate time, with a fixed entanglement target, I prove
that the cost-minimizing schedule of exchange angles is unique and has
closed form. Quiet layers receive more entanglement, and layers whose
noise rate passes a threshold set by the target receive none.

In eight-layer density-matrix simulations, this schedule improves
final-state fidelity by up to 0.040 over a single full entangler and by
0.025 over equal angles when noise varies between layers. The per-gate
behavior agrees with published continuous-gate results on
superconducting hardware. These gains assume the exchange angle can be
calibrated directly; where the gate has to be built from fixed native
gates, they may shrink or disappear.
